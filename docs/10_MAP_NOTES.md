# The three maps — what I built, and what I still need from you

The maps live in `sim/maps/*.txt` and are generated by
`sim/maps/build_maps.py`. Passages are **30 cm** everywhere, sector lines are
**30 cm straight across the passage**, and the start and finish zones are
outside the maze — all as you measured.

The grid is **3 cm per character**, so a passage is 10 characters and a wall
is 1. Real walls are about 2 cm, so each map comes out a few cm bigger overall
than the tape measure says. That does not matter: the passage width is exact,
and the passage width is what decides everything.

## How to correct a map yourself

The `.txt` files are plain characters — `#` is wall, `.` is floor. Open one in
Notepad, move a wall, save, and run:

```bash
cd sim
python3 run.py --map maps/map1.txt --compare
```

Or edit the dimensions in `sim/maps/build_maps.py` and re-run
`python3 build_maps.py`. Everything is in centimetres in that file.

## What the maps told us

Running the real robot size (25 × 15 cm) against these three maps at 30 cm
passages:

| Map | Sector points | Time | Wall scrapes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Map 2 island loop | **8 / 8** | 38 s | 11 |
| Map 1 serpentine | 3–4 / 8 | ran out of time | 100–270 |
| Map 3 spine snake | 1–2 / 8 | ran out of time | 18–230 |

Map 2 is all long straights with four corners, and the robot handles it.
Maps 1 and 3 have corners closer together, and that is where a 25 cm robot in
a 30 cm passage runs out of room — it sweeps a **29.2 cm circle** when it
pivots, leaving **4 mm each side**. The scrape counts are the robot grinding
its way round corners it barely fits through.

Measured effect of making the robot smaller, across all three maps and both
directions:

| Robot | Swept circle | Maps solved | Sector points | Scrapes per run |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 250 × 150 mm *(yours today)* | 292 mm | 56 % | 63 % | 100 |
| 230 × 150 mm | 275 mm | 50 % | 58 % | 94 |
| 210 × 150 mm | 258 mm | 44 % | 59 % | 85 |
| 210 × 130 mm | 247 mm | 56 % | 66 % | 84 |
| **190 × 130 mm** | **230 mm** | **75 %** | **72 %** | 73 |

Shrinking the robot is worth real points. Getting it under about
**21 × 13 cm** is the target. That means: nothing hanging off the front or
back, sensors tucked inside the wheel line, battery stacked on top rather
than behind the axle, and the wheel axle exactly in the middle of the body.

The remaining failures on maps 1 and 3 are not all geometry — some are the
navigation logic needing more work on tight back-to-back corners. That is the
next job, and it is easier to do once the map shapes are confirmed, which is
why the photos above would help.
